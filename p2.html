National Trend Analysis: Total Incidence vs. Fatality Rates

A comparative longitudinal analysis of national heat illness data between 2025 and 2026 reveals a concerning asynchronous trend between morbidity and mortality. While total patient volume decreased by approximately 7.9% (from 3,885 to 3,576), the national fatality count rose by 33.3% (from 24 to 32). This statistical divergence indicates that while the absolute reach of heat-related events may have contracted, the clinical severity of cases intensified significantly.

From an epidemiological perspective, the most critical metric is the Case Fatality Rate (CFR), which climbed from 0.62% in 2025 to 0.89% in 2026. This inverse relationship—lower volume coupled with higher lethality—suggests a shift in the environmental or demographic risk profile. For public health policy, this necessitates a transition from broad awareness campaigns to high-acuity clinical triage protocols, as the 2026 data reflects a higher concentration of life-threatening conditions, such as heatstroke, over less severe heat exhaustion.

Primary Metropolitan Analysis

Gyeonggi (Highest Volume Region) Gyeonggi continues to represent the largest regional burden (composition ratio rose from 23.1% to 24.7%). However, it demonstrated remarkable clinical resilience: despite 885 patients in 2026, fatalities dropped from 5 to 2, reducing CFR from 0.56% to 0.23%.
Seoul (The Growing Concern) Seoul saw a 37.3% surge in patients (327 to 449) and a doubling of fatalities (3 to 6). Its composition share expanded from 8.4% to 12.6%, and CFR escalated from 0.92% to 1.34%, signaling urban heat island risks and geriatric vulnerability.

Regional Mortality Shifts

Jeonbuk Mortality Spike Fatalities rose sharply from 1 to 6 despite a stable patient count (215 to 223), resulting in a localized CFR of 2.69%—nearly triple the national average, marking acute care vulnerabilities.
Proactive Mitigation Models Gyeongbuk halved its deaths (4 to 2). Chungnam achieved complete elimination of heat deaths (5 to 0), serving as a benchmark model for decoupling volume from mortality.
